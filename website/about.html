About CostPlusDB

Built by a bootstrapped trucker who got tired of cloud provider price gouging.
No VC funding. No exit strategy. Just honest pricing.

The Story

I'm Jeremy. I run a small software consultancy (intent solutions io) while driving a truck part-time to keep the lights on.

I kept seeing the same pattern with my consulting clients:

  1. Build an app or SaaS product
  2. Launch on AWS/GCP
  3. Get hit with a $500-2000/month database bill
  4. Panic and try to optimize
  5. Realize the problem is the 2000% markup, not their usage

I'd help them migrate to cheaper VPS providers (Contabo, Hetzner, DigitalOcean) and save them $400-1800/month. Every single time, they'd ask:

"Why doesn't someone just... do this as a service?"

So I did.

Why "Cost Plus 25%"?

I could've done 50%. Or 100%. Or the industry standard of 2000%.

But here's the thing: 25% is enough.

  • Covers my time for setup, monitoring, support
  • Pays for automation tooling
  • Lets me save up for our own hardware (see below)
  • Fair for you, sustainable for me

If I wanted to get rich quick, I'd be working at a FAANG company, not driving a truck. This is about building something long-term and honest.

Long-Term Vision: Our Own Hardware

Right now, I rent VPS servers from providers like Contabo and Hetzner. That's where most of your monthly cost goes.

Goal: Within 12-18 months, own our servers in a colocation datacenter.

  • Use Contabo/Hetzner/DigitalOcean/AWS
  • Build customer base
  • Refine operations and automation
  • Save 25% revenue for hardware fund
  • Document everything (you can see our SOPs!)

Status: ✅ Active now

  • Cost: ~$8,000 upfront + $200/month colo fees
  • Capacity: 50-100 databases

Your benefit: Monthly price drops 30-40%

Timeline: Q2-Q3 2026

Phase 3: Multi-Region (Month 18-36)
  • Add second datacenter (EU or Asia)
  • Offer high-availability across regions
  • Still 25% markup, just on lower hardware costs

Timeline: 2027

Why Tell You This?

Transparency. You're trusting me with your database. You deserve to know the plan.

When we switch to owned hardware:

  1. Your price goes down (same 25% markup, lower costs)
  2. You see the new breakdown on your invoice
  3. No service interruption (we migrate during your maintenance window)
  4. You get better performance (dedicated hardware vs shared VPS)

Customer Selection

I'm building this slowly and carefully. I pick my customers.

Good Fit

  • Indie developers - Building SaaS, tools, or side projects
  • Startups - Pre-seed to Series A, watching burn rate
  • Small businesses - Need a database, not enterprise complexity
  • Bootstrappers - Value honesty over sales pitches
  • Long-term thinkers - Want a partner, not a vendor

Not a Good Fit

  • Enterprise with 99.999% SLA needs - I offer 99.9% (measured monthly)
  • 24/7 phone support required - I do email, 4-hour SLA during business hours
  • Hand-holding - I assume you know PostgreSQL basics
  • Price shoppers - If you'll leave for $5/month savings, we're not a fit
  • High churn tolerance - I want customers for years, not months

I reserve the right to decline customers. This keeps quality high and lets me maintain the 4-hour support SLA.
